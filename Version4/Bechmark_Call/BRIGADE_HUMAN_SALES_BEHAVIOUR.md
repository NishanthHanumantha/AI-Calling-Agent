## 3. Strongest Human-Sales Behaviours

### 3.1 Opening

The opening is a short, conventional introduction: good evening, name, company, and the reason for the call (an enquiry). It does not start with an amenities or price list.

The customer then corrects the frame of the call: they want South Bengaluru and only selected an available enquiry. The salesperson does not keep pitching the enquired project. The next move is a location question. The useful part of the opening is how quickly it reaches the customer's requirement, not the greeting formula.

### 3.2 Qualification

The first question is location. The second is configuration. Each one uses the previous answer.

That order is useful. Location decides whether a project is even relevant. Configuration decides which home to describe. Detailed size, price, and completion come only after both answers.

The two questions are direct, so this stretch feels like a short interview. It does not feel like a long questionnaire. Only two facts are gathered before the project explanation: the locality the customer named, and a two-bedroom requirement.

### 3.3 Listening and acknowledgement

|------------------|-------------|----------|-----------|
| Wants South Bengaluru, not simply the project they clicked | Does not continue with the original enquiry | Asks which location | Waits |
| Names a locality | "OK", then a configuration question | Does not describe the project yet | Asks configuration |
| Says two-bedroom | Uses that configuration | Describes a two-bedroom option and a nearby landmark | Gives size and price |
| Asks the distance | First reply misses it and continues product description | Customer asks again | Asks which part of the locality, then checks |
| Asks the price again | Answers with the price | Does not add a new topic | Stops |
| Restates the completion month | Confirms the month | Corrects the unclear year | Stops |

Short acknowledgements ("OK", "yeah", "no worries") are frequent. The stronger listening is later in the call, when a missed question is asked again and then answered.

### 3.4 Tailored explanation

After the customer says two-bedroom and names a locality, the explanation is about one project near a landmark, a two-bedroom size range, and a price. It is not a tour of every project type.

The explanation is only partly tailored. Before the distance is answered, the salesperson also adds community type, site size, unit count, and completion. Those facts were not what the customer had just asked. The transcript's exact numbers for area, unit count, and price should not be reused. The behaviour is what matters: one relevant project, then more facts than the question required.

When the customer later asks price, distance, offer, and possession, the replies narrow to that item.

### 3.5 Question sequencing

Early questions are one at a time and follow the last answer: location, then configuration.

The customer is not asked budget, purpose, or a site visit. "Anything else" is asked once, at the end, not after every answer.

The distance section shows a useful recovery. The salesperson does not know the distance, says they have nothing in that locality, then hears that the customer wants distance rather than a project inside the locality. The next question is which part of the locality. After a pause, one distance is given.

### 3.6 Objection handling

The clearest concern is location, not price.

The customer asks how far the project is. The first response does not answer it. The customer points that out. The salesperson does not argue and does not say the location is fine anyway. They ask a narrower location question, take time to check, and then give about 12 kilometres.

A second concern is an offer. The exact customer wording is unclear. The salesperson checks and says there is no offer because the project is newly launched. They do not invent an offer.

The salesperson does not dismiss these points. The weak moment is the first distance reply, which continues the project description instead of answering the question.

### 3.7 Closing

The close starts only after location, configuration, price, distance, offer, and possession have been discussed. The question is whether the customer is comfortable with this property, not a request to book a visit.

The customer says they need to check and will come back. The salesperson accepts that, offers to share details, and asks whether that next step is all right. The customer agrees. One "anything else" follows, then thanks.

The close is not forced. Commitment is left with the customer.

## 5. Reusable Human-Sales Behaviour Rules

These are supported by this recording. They are behaviours, not lines to copy.

1. Keep the opening short and reach the customer's requirement quickly.
2. Ask location before describing a project.
3. Ask configuration before describing a home.
4. Use the previous answer in the next question or explanation.
5. Ask one question, then wait.
6. Answer the question the customer just asked before adding other project facts.
7. If a fact is not known, say so, ask a narrower question, and check.
8. Do not invent an offer or a distance.
9. Confirm a fact the customer repeats, briefly.
10. Ask "anything else" once, near the end, not after every answer.
11. If the customer wants to think, accept that and offer to share details.
12. Do not introduce a site visit before the customer is comfortable with the property.

This recording does not support a rule that the salesperson never gives extra facts. After the configuration answer, several unrelated facts were given before the distance was answered.

## 6. Anti-Patterns to Avoid

Supported by this recording, by the current Version 4 call flow, or by both.

| Anti-pattern | Evidence |
|--------------|----------|
| Answering a different topic from the one just asked | In the recording, the first distance question is followed by more project description. In Version 4, a specific statement can become a keyword intent and a canned project answer. |
| Giving several project facts before the customer's question is answered | The recording does this once, between about 0:59 and 1:36. Version 4 knowledge-base answers do it by design. |
| A fixed menu instead of the customer's requirement | Not in this recording. Version 4, after a yes to the greeting, always asks about amenities, pricing, location, or floor plans. |
| The same follow-up after every answer | Not in this recording. "Anything else" happens once at the end. Version 4 appends an "anything else or site visit" line after FAQ answers. |
| A site visit before the customer is comfortable | Not in this recording. Version 4 can move to a visit ask from the FAQ follow-up and from the stage machine. |
| Inventing a missing commercial fact | The salesperson in this call checks and says there is no offer. Version 4's "do not invent" rule already points the same way when the model is used. |
| Arguing with a location concern | Not done in this call. The concern is eventually measured in kilometres. |

## 7. Version4 Gap Analysis

Version 4's live path is still `handle_speech()`, `detect_intent()`, `answer_from_kb()`, `generate_response()`, and `answer_faq_and_prompt()` in `app_v3.py`. The language model is used only for some FAQ answers. Greeting, the qualification menu, visit booking, and closing are fixed.

### A. Behaviours Version 4 already supports

- A short opening that names the project and asks if this is a good time.
- A limit of one or two sentences when the FAQ model is actually called.
- A refusal to invent facts, in the FAQ prompt and in the knowledge-base fallback.
- A later visit-booking path with a day and fixed time slots, if the customer agrees.
- One follow-up question after an FAQ answer.

### B. Behaviours Version 4 currently lacks

- Asking the customer's locality before any project description.
- Asking configuration before describing homes.
- Answering distance, price, offer, or possession as the whole reply.
- Saying a fact is unknown, asking a narrower question, and then answering.
- Accepting "I need to check" by offering to share details.
- Keeping "anything else" to the end of the call.
- Holding the site visit until the customer is comfortable.

### C. Behaviours that can be improved inside the existing FAQ model call

These fit the current single Sarvam call in `generate_response()` and the follow-up built in `answer_faq_and_prompt()`:

- Acknowledge the customer's point.
- Answer that point before any extra fact.
- Ask one next question based on the last answer.
- Remove the repeated "anything else or site visit" suffix from every FAQ turn.
- If the supplied project information does not contain the fact, say so instead of substituting a general project description.

### D. Behaviours that need a larger change

- Looking something up during the call, as in the pause before the 12 kilometre answer. Version 4 has no lookup step.
- Remembering locality and configuration across turns. `conversation_memory` does not store the customer's words.
- Replacing the fixed greeting and qualification menu.
- A details-sharing close instead of the current visit or goodbye branches.
- Deciding inside the model when a visit may be offered. That would move stage control out of `handle_speech()`.

## 8. Recommended Behaviour Rules for Human-Like V1

For a later FAQ-only change, these rules are the ones this recording supports. Do not implement them in this phase.

1. Respond to the customer's last point before any other project fact.
2. If location or configuration is still unknown, ask that one question and do not describe the project yet.
3. Use the locality and configuration already given. Do not restart with a generic topic menu.
4. Ask one relevant question. Do not add "anything else" or a site visit after every answer.
5. If the required fact is not in the supplied project information, say it is not available. Do not fill the gap with amenities or a general location sentence.
6. If the customer wants time to decide, accept that and offer to share details.
7. Leave visit booking in the existing stage code. Do not let the FAQ reply invent a visit close.
